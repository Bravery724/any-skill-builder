# Weekly Team Update

Create an update that lets someone outside the immediate project understand what changed, why it matters, and what needs attention. Use the supplied notes and linked material the user has authorized you to read. Do not send or publish the update unless asked.

## Read the notes

- Identify the reporting period, intended readers, completed work, work in progress, next actions, and unresolved decisions. Reuse information already supplied.
- Keep completed work separate from targets, estimates, proposals, and blocked work. A merged change is not necessarily deployed; a staged test is not production evidence.
- Attribute owners, dates, metrics, and outcomes only when the notes support them. Preserve the scope and units of measurements. Do not infer a commitment from a tentative note.
- When notes conflict, retain the uncertainty explicitly. Do not silently prefer the most optimistic statement or the latest-looking fragment unless the input establishes its authority.

## Draft with visible gaps

The selected workflow is to produce a useful draft first. Mark missing owners, dates, or conflicting status plainly beside the affected item. Ask only the focused follow-up that is needed to resolve a material gap; do not block the whole draft for a minor omission.

Write naturally in the conversation's language unless another output language is requested. Lead with the most useful change or unresolved decision. Use brief paragraphs and a few bullets when they help scanning; do not force empty sections or fill space with generic optimism.

Explain project terms that cross-functional readers would not recognize. For each important change, connect the action to its supported consequence. If the notes do not establish the consequence, say what was done without inventing a benefit.

Keep progress, next actions, and requests for help easy to distinguish. A request for help should explain what is needed and what it affects. Include an owner or due date only when supplied; otherwise state that it is unconfirmed.

## Preserve useful evidence

Put supplied source links beside the claim they support and use descriptive link text. A reader should understand the point before opening the link. Preserve identifiers such as issue numbers and links accurately. If a link was not opened, do not claim to have verified its destination.

Before delivering, check that:

- every factual statement is supported by the notes or inspected material;
- no target, proposal, or contradiction became a completed result;
- the reader can identify the practical next action or unresolved decision;
- the language sounds like a helpful teammate and keeps necessary uncertainty.

Return the draft itself. Add a short clarification request only when unresolved information materially changes its meaning. Do not append a mechanical checklist of your internal checks.
